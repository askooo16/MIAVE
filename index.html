About the Place

One island, a thousand green hills

Bohol sits in the central Visayas, southeast of Cebu. Its rolling interior holds the famous Chocolate Hills, its rivers run through coconut and rice country, and its southwest coast reaches the white beaches of Panglao Island.

The Chocolate Hills were declared a National Geological Monument in 1988. Locals and visitors also come for the Philippine tarsier, centuries-old stone churches and the calm cruises along the Loboc River.

Activities

A day across the island

Most visitors do a loop from Tagbilaran, with a river lunch in the middle. Here is a common route in order.

  1. Start

    Tagbilaran City

    The capital and main port. Ferries from Cebu arrive here, and most island tours begin from its hotels and the pier.

  2. Church stop

    Baclayon Church

    One of the oldest stone churches in the country, facing the sea. It was damaged in the 2013 earthquake and has since been restored.

  3. Midday

    Loboc River

    A slow cruise past nipa palms and jungle banks, with a buffet lunch and live music on board.

  4. Wildlife

    Tarsier sanctuary

    A short walk through forest to see the Philippine tarsier, a tiny nocturnal primate with huge eyes.

  5. Finish

    Chocolate Hills

    Climb the viewing deck in Carmen for a wide view of the grassy mounds that stretch to the horizon.

Land and sea

In the forest

Tarsiers sleep in the trees by day. Keep your voice low, never use flash and never touch them, since they stress easily.

Along the road

The man-made mahogany forest in Bilar forms a long green tunnel over the highway, planted decades ago and now a favorite photo stop.

Under the water

Panglao and nearby Balicasag Island offer reefs for snorkeling and diving, and dolphin watching trips leave early in the morning.

On the water

Kayaking

Local operators run paddle tours on calmer stretches of Bohol's rivers, where you drift past palms and quiet banks.

Cruise

The Loboc River cruise is the classic way to see the river, with lunch on board and music along the way.

Food and Culture

Taste and tradition

Local sweets

Calamay, a sticky sweet made from coconut milk and glutinous rice, is sold in coconut-shell containers. Peanut kisses are another popular treat to take home.

Fresh seafood

Beach restaurants in Panglao and Tagbilaran grill the day's catch, and many serve it with rice and fresh coconut.

History and faith

Old stone churches such as Baclayon show the island's Spanish-era history. The Sandugo, or blood compact of 1565, is still remembered in Bohol today.

Travel Guide

Plan your visit

Getting there

Fly into Bohol-Panglao International Airport, or take a fast ferry from Cebu City to Tagbilaran. From there, vans and tricycles cover the island.

When to go

The drier months, roughly January to May, bring clear skies. In the dry season the hills turn brown, which is how they got their name.

What to bring

Sunscreen, water, a hat, light clothes and comfortable shoes for the steps up to the viewpoints. Bring cash for small entrance fees.

Check before you go. Fees, opening hours and river cruise schedules change. Confirm with the Bohol provincial tourism office or a licensed tour operator before you travel.

Accommodations

Where to stay

Panglao Island

Beach resorts and guesthouses around Alona Beach suit divers and anyone who wants to be near the water.

Tagbilaran City

City hotels near the port are handy for ferry arrivals and early island tours.

Countryside

Smaller lodges near Loboc and Carmen put you close to the river and the Chocolate Hills. Book ahead in busy months.

Bookings

Room

Book a room

Choose from beach resorts, city hotels and countryside lodges. Reserve early for the dry season, when the island is busiest.

Island hopping

Book a boat tour

Island hopping trips from Panglao visit spots such as Balicasag Island, with time for snorkeling and the beach.

Promo

Ask about promos

Hotels and tour operators sometimes offer seasonal packages. Ask when you book, since offers change.

Events

Festivals and fiestas

The Sandugo Festival in Tagbilaran, usually held in July, celebrates the 1565 blood compact with parades, street dancing and cultural shows. Towns across the island also hold their own fiestas during the year, each honoring a patron saint.

Dates change from year to year, so check with the Bohol provincial tourism office before you plan around an event.
